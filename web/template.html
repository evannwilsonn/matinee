        How it was tested

        Every MovieLens user had a random 20% of their ratings hidden. Each model then had to pick 10 films per user from the roughly 9,700 they hadn't rated in training, and a pick counted when the user had given the hidden film 4 stars or more.

        Good at ratings, bad at picks

        Models trained to predict star ratings got the lowest error ( RMSE), but their top 10s almost never held a film the user went on to rate highly. They lost to simply recommending the most-rated films.

        What fixed it

        Implicit ALS learns from which films people rated, with stars as confidence. Its precision@10 is against for most-popular, and its picks cover × more of the catalogue.

        Checked against Spark

        The Scala ALS here is written from scratch. Spark MLlib's ALS on the same data and settings lands within on NDCG@10. The deployed batch job uses Spark itself.

        What the demo leaves out

        It only offers films at least people rated, so the factors behind each one are trustworthy. New and niche films are left out.
