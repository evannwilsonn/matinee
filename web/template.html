        How it was tested

        Each of 610 MovieLens members had a random 20% of their ratings hidden. Every model then picked 10 films per member from about 9,700 they hadn't rated, and a pick counted when the member had given the hidden film 4 stars or more.

        The rating predictors were the wrong tool. Models built to guess star ratings had the lowest error ( RMSE), but their top 10s almost never held a film the member went on to rate highly. Just recommending the most-rated films beat them.

        Learning from what people watch fixed it. Implicit ALS treats every rating as "watched this", with stars as confidence. Its picks land more often than most-popular's, and come from × more of the catalogue.

        Checked against Spark. The Scala ALS is written from scratch and lands within of Spark MLlib's on NDCG@10. The batch job that writes every member's picks runs on Spark itself.

        What isn't on the shelf. Only titles at least people rated, so every recommendation rests on enough data.
